U.S. Tax Instructions (2025 tax year)

A plain-English walkthrough for U.S. filers — every item references the Internal Revenue Code (IRC) or IRS publications so you can verify it yourself.

1. How a refund actually happens

A refund simply means you overpaid during the year — through payroll withholding (Box 2 of your W-2) or quarterly estimated tax (Form 1040-ES, IRC §6654). Filing Form 1040 is how you get the excess back.

After e-filing, refunds typically arrive within 21 days. Track yours at IRS.gov/refunds ("Where's My Refund?").IRC §6402

2. Who must file — and who should anyway

Whether you must file depends on filing status, age, and gross income.IRC §6012IRS Pub 17 But even if you're below the threshold: if any tax was withheld from your pay, filing is the only way to get it refunded.

Filing statuses: Single, Married Filing Jointly, Married Filing Separately, Head of Household, Qualifying Surviving Spouse. Choosing Head of Household when eligible (you paid over half the cost of keeping up a home for a qualifying person) can save thousands.

3. Refundable credits — money even with zero tax

Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC). The amount depends on earned income, filing status, qualifying-child or age/residency rules, and annual income and investment limits. A low income bracket by itself does not establish eligibility. Check the IRS EITC Assistant or Pub. 596.IRC §32IRS Pub 596

Child Tax Credit. Up to $2,200 per child under 17 (2025), of which up to $1,700 is refundable. Phase-outs start at $200k MAGI single / $400k joint. Non-child dependents (e.g., college students) can qualify for the $500 Credit for Other Dependents. File Schedule 8812.IRC §24

American Opportunity Credit. Up to $2,500 per student for the first 4 years of college — 40% of it refundable.IRC §25AForm 8863

4. Above-the-line deductions — no itemizing needed

Some eligible adjustments reduce income whether or not you itemize. Each has separate requirements:

• Student loan interest — a possible deduction only for interest actually paid on a qualified loan, subject to filing-status, dependency, and annual MAGI rules. The deduction is limited to the lesser of $2,500 or qualified interest paid; verify the tax-year rules at IRS.gov.IRC §221

• Traditional IRA contributions — may be deductible depending on filing status, income, and workplace-plan coverage. Roth IRA contributions are not deductible from current income. Verify the applicable tax-year rules at IRS.gov.IRC §219IRS Pub 590-A

• HSA contributions — up to $4,300 self-only / $8,550 family (2025); triple tax-advantaged.IRC §223

• Educator expenses — $300 of classroom supplies for teachers.IRC §62(a)(2)(D)

5. Standard vs. itemized deduction

2025 standard deduction: $15,750 single / $31,500 married joint / $23,625 head of household. Additional standard deduction for those 65+ or blind.IRC §63Rev. Proc. 2024-40

Itemize (Schedule A) only if your total exceeds that: mortgage interest, state & local taxes capped at $10,000, charitable gifts, and medical expenses over 7.5% of AGI.§164(b)(6)§213§170

Close to the line? "Bunching" two years of charitable giving into one year can push you over.

6. Self-employed & gig workers

Business expenses (mileage at $0.70/mile for 2025, supplies, software, phone share, health premiums under §162(l)) reduce Schedule C income. You also deduct half your self-employment tax.IRC §162§164(f)IRS Pub 334

Net earnings of $400 or more generally require you to file a return and pay self-employment tax (Schedule SE). Separately, if you expect to owe $1,000 or more in tax after withholding, you must pay it through quarterly estimated taxes (Form 1040-ES) — not just when self-employed. The home office deduction ($5/sq ft simplified, up to $1,500) requires regular and exclusive business use.IRC §6017§6654IRC §280AIRS Pub 587

7. Deadlines, extensions, and the 3-year refund window

Federal returns are due April 15. Form 4868 extends filing to October 15 — but not the time to pay; unpaid balances accrue interest and penalties.IRC §6081§6651

Critical: to claim a refund you generally must file within 3 years of the later of the original due date or the date you actually filed — and no later than 2 years from the date you paid the tax, whichever is later. (These periods can differ for amended returns, foreign filers, and certain disasters.) The IRS may hold a refund if you didn't file a required prior-year return.IRC §6511(a)

Made a mistake on a filed return? Amend with Form 1040-X — generally within 3 years of filing the original.

8. Free filing & free in-person help

IRS Free File — free guided software for eligible taxpayers: irs.gov/freefile.

VITA / Tax Counseling for the Elderly — free in-person preparation (generally ≤$67k income, disabilities, or 60+): irs.gov/vita or 1-800-906-9887.

Identity protection — get an IP PIN each year so nobody files a fraudulent return in your name: irs.gov/ippin.

If you can't pay what you owe, the IRS offers installment agreements and Offers in Compromise — never ignore notices.IRC §6159

9. Warning: penalties for false claims

Claiming deductions or credits you don't qualify for carries a 20% accuracy penalty, 75% for fraud, plus potential criminal liability — up to 5 years for tax evasion.IRC §6662§6663§7201

When in doubt, keep records and ask an IRS-authorized professional (CPA, Enrolled Agent, or tax attorney — check credentials at irs.treasury.gov/rpo).

Frequently asked questions

What tax deductions could I be missing?

Possible review areas include student-loan interest, state sales tax when applicable, educator expenses, HSA contributions, and the deductible portion of self-employment tax. Employee job-search costs are generally not a federal deduction under current law. Take the 8-question check above to see which items may need review.

What are the most common missed tax deductions?

Potential itemized deductions can include qualifying charitable gifts, medical expenses above the applicable AGI threshold, mortgage interest, and certain energy-related items. Each has separate requirements; compare the eligible total with the standard deduction and verify current IRS rules.

What records should I keep?

Keep receipts, statements, mileage logs, and other records that support each claim. Forms such as 1098-E and 5498-SA help document certain items, but a form does not automatically prove eligibility. The IRS can ask for substantiation.

What if I forgot to include a tax deduction?

You may be able to file Form 1040-X, but the deadline depends on the refund-claim rules and the date tax was paid. Check the current IRS instructions and IRC §6511 before relying on a three-year period.

What can I write off on my taxes self-employed?

Self-employed filers may deduct ordinary and necessary business expenses, subject to records, limits, and the correct form. Home office, equipment, software, health insurance, and retirement items each have separate rules. Answer the quiz questions to get a review list.

What are the biggest tax deductions for 2025?

The answer depends on filing status and the law in effect for tax year 2025. Compare the official standard deduction with potentially itemized mortgage interest, state and local taxes, charitable gifts, and medical expenses. Verify current amounts at IRS.gov before relying on them.
